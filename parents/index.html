The Cultural Calendar

What the whole school is marking this week

Every week the school marks a day or a festival, with ideas to try and sometimes a game. The idea above works at home too, if anyone fancies it. Nothing needs doing or sending in.

The timetable

The same shape every week this half term.

Days run 9:30 to 2:30. The times are a guide rather than orders: if a session is not working for someone on a given day, that is fine to say, and there is always another way to do it.

Daily rhythms

Every day starts and ends the same way, on purpose.

Knowing what is coming makes a day easier to walk into, so the shape of the day does not change. The calm start and the calm finish are as much a part of the timetable as maths.

    How it works

    The website Base 5 uses every day.

    Their own space

    Every student has an HQ of their own, with its own look and name, holding English and maths in short chunks pitched at where they are. It saves as they go, and anything they try counts.

    The plan for the day

    The home page shows what is on now and next, and each day has its own page. Knowing what is coming helps, so it is always one tap away.

    Games, earned

    There are lots of games, many made for the group. Inside an HQ, a game is earned by having a go at some learning first, then it takes a short break, so the learning stays the main thing.

    Everything on the site is written as an invitation rather than an instruction: "have a go if you fancy it", "nothing in here is marked", "sit this one out". Students choose the order, can skip, can ask to be shown the answer, and can stop whenever they like. There is a Done for now button on every HQ page that simply gives them a calm screen.

    This half term the whole group is working on one big question: If we could time travel, should we? Each student answers it in their own way, through a project of their choosing.

    Keeping everyone safe

    How the website is built to protect students.

    Worried about something?

    Please say. It is never a bother.
    999If a child is in immediate danger, call 999 straight away.

    If you are worried about the safety or wellbeing of any child at Chilton Bridge, please contact the school's safeguarding team as soon as you can, through the school office. You can also tell Jay, who will pass it to the safeguarding team straight away.

    Out of school hours

    • The NSPCC helpline, for adults worried about a child: 0808 800 5000, or help@nspcc.org.uk.
    • Your local council's children's services can also take concerns about a child.
    • Young people can talk to Childline for free on 0800 1111.

    Questions

    Always welcome.

    Get in touch with Jay at jay.raine@chiltonbridgeschool.co.uk, or through the school office. To see your child's own progress, ask them to show you on their device, or ask Jay.

    Look around

    As a guest.

    You are very welcome to look around the website as a guest:

    • You can open every page, play the games and try any activity.
    • Nothing you do is saved or sent. It lasts until you close the tab.
    • No student's own work is shown. An HQ opens fresh, as it would for someone brand new, and each student's private folder stays closed.
