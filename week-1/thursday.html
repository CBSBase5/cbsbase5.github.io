Roughly what is about today. Coming in and settling. Something to eat. The three things above, in whatever order suits. A walk out at some point if the weather holds. Home time.

None of that is a running order and none of it is compulsory. If you want to sit and do nothing for the first hour, that is a completely reasonable way to come back after six weeks off.

Two truths and a lie

Think of three things about yourself. Two of them are true. One is completely made up. Everyone else has to work out which one is the lie.

The good ones are usually specific. "I have been to Spain" is hard to guess at. "I once got stuck in a lift with a man dressed as a wizard" is much better, whether or not it happened.

Things that tend to work: something you are unexpectedly good at, somewhere strange you have been, a thing that happened to a pet, an injury and how you got it, a food you genuinely like that other people find upsetting.

Other ways to do this

  • Write your three down and let Jay read them out, so you are not the one talking.
  • Do it with one other person instead of the whole group.
  • Just do the guessing and skip having a turn.
  • Make all three up. Nobody will know, and it is funnier.
  • Sit this one out entirely. It will not be mentioned again.

A user manual for you

Everything else comes with instructions. People do not, which is why so much of this goes wrong. So write yours.

This is not a worksheet and there is no right length. One line is a fine user manual. So is three pages. Answer the ones that interest you and ignore the rest.

  • How do you like people to get your attention?
  • What does it look like from the outside when you have had enough?
  • What actually helps at that point, and what makes it worse?
  • What do you want someone to know before they ask you to do something?
  • What could you talk about for an hour without getting bored?
  • What is the worst thing a person could say to you, even if they meant well?
  • What do people usually get wrong about you?

Who sees it

  • You decide. It can go on the wall, it can go to Jay only, or it can stay with you and nobody reads it.
  • You can change it whenever you like. People are not fixed and neither is this.
  • Typing it, writing it, recording it, or drawing it all count.

The Question Wall

There is a big sheet of paper on the wall. Write a question on it. Anyone in Base 5 can write an answer underneath, including Jay.

No names, unless you want to put one. Questions can be serious, daft, or genuinely useful. Past ones have ranged from what happens if you swallow a magnet to whether Jay has ever been in trouble with the police.

It stays up all week, so you do not have to think of something now.

If you would rather not write on the wall

  • Hand a question to Jay on a scrap of paper and it will go up without your name.
  • Answer other people's questions and never ask one. That is half the point of it anyway.
