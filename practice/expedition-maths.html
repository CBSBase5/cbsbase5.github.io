Pack the bag

Bonds to 10, then the same bonds with tens, hundreds and thousands.

Learn a bitskip it if you know this already

Same bond, bigger pieces. If 3 and 7 make 10, then 3 hundreds and 7 hundreds make 10 hundreds, which is 1,000.

It works for taking away too: 1,000 − 300 is 10 hundreds take away 3 hundreds, so 7 hundreds, which is 700.

Water top up

What makes a number up to 100? Fill it to the brim, not over.

Learn a bitskip it if you know this already

Hop to the next ten first, then jump to 100 in tens. Add the hops.

Quick check: the ones make 10 and the tens make 9. For 64 and 36: 4 + 6 = 10 and 6 + 3 = 9.

Trail steps

Add and take away two digit numbers in your head. Every answer is a step up the hill.

Learn a bitthree handy ways, pick your favourite

Taking away works the same way. For 82 − 47 you can jump back (82 − 40 = 42, then 42 − 7 = 35) or count up from 47 to 82 (3 to 50, 30 to 80, 2 to 82, so 35).

Hire hut

Formulas written in words. A number goes in, the steps happen in order, a number comes out.

Learn a bitskip it if you know this already

“Cost = 5 pounds per hour plus 3 pounds” means: hours × 5 first, then add 3. Same order every time.

Working backwards? Undo the steps in reverse: take away 3, then divide by 5.

Map room

Measure on the map, then turn it into real distances. Then plan the route.

Learn a bitskip it if you know this already

1 : 25,000 means every 1 cm on the map is 25,000 cm on the ground. 25,000 cm is 250 m.

This is the scale of the Ordnance Survey Explorer maps that walkers use. Each blue grid square is 4 cm across, which is 1 km.
