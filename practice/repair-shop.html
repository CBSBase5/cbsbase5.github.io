THE REPAIR SHOP

Digital media

Broken computers come in. Happy customers go out. You hold the screwdriver.

Welcome to the workshop

Every part has a job. Find the one that stopped doing it.

Customers bring in poorly computers and tell you what is wrong. You look inside, work out which part has failed, swap it, and write the repair note.

Before the shop opens there is a back room for learning the parts, if you fancy it. Six stages, any order, skip anything. It saves as you go.

Stage 3 · the test bench

Follow the data

No part works alone. Type on the keyboard below and watch your key press travel through the computer, part to part.

Learn a bit The desk and the filing cabinet

The CPU is the worker. It carries out instructions and does all the working out.

RAM is the desk. The things you are using right now sit there so the worker can reach them fast. At home time the desk is cleared: RAM forgets everything when the power goes off. That is called volatile.

Storage (an SSD or hard disk drive) is the filing cabinet. It keeps things with no power at all. That is called non volatile.

Stage 4 · the shop is open

The repair shop

Learn a bit How a repair detective works

1. Listen to what the customer says is wrong.

2. Think: which part has that job?

3. Tap that part and swap in a new one. If the problem is still there, that part was fine, so you have ruled it out. That counts as detective work, not a mistake.

4. Write a one sentence repair note.

Try a bit Pick a day, pick a customer

Stage 5 · the front counter

Sales counter

Some old customers are back. This time they want a new computer. Compare the two on the counter and pick the one that suits the job.

Learn a bit Reading a spec sheet

Processor: more cores and more GHz means more working out every second.

Memory (RAM): more GB means more programs and tabs open at once without slowing down.

Storage: more GB or TB means more room for files. 1 TB is 1,000 GB. An SSD is much faster than a hard disk drive.

Graphics card: draws detailed pictures fast. Big games and video editing love one. Emails do not need one.
