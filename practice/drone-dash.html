Maths

Run a drone delivery company: fly by vectors, dodge no fly zones, flip parcels into the van and build landing pads.

Welcome to the depot

You are the boss now

Your company delivers parcels by drone. The drones only understand column vectors. The parcels only fit the van if they are flipped and turned just right. And there is a sale on in the shop.

Seven stages. Pick any one, in any order, and skip anything that is not for you.

Who is this? (optional)

Stage 1 · Flight school

Drones speak vector

Read the vector for a flight, type one to fly, and find where a vector lands.

Learn a bit

A column vector is two numbers stacked in tall brackets.

Top: across. Right is positive, left is negative.
Bottom: up or down. Up is positive, down is negative.

So 54 is 5 right and 4 up, and -30 is 3 left and no up or down.

Stage 2 · No fly zones

Plan the route

Add two flights together, then plan routes round the red zones.

Learn a bit

Two flights in a row add up to one flight. Add the top numbers, then add the bottom numbers.

24 then 5-3 is the same as 71 in one go.

A drone cannot fly through a red no fly zone, so sometimes it takes the long way.

Stage 3 · The parcel shop

Sale on in the drone shop

Every discount is a multiple of 5%. Build it from blocks, then price it to the penny.

Learn a bit

10%: divide by 10. 5%: halve the 10%.

Every multiple of 5% is built from those blocks. 25% = 10% + 10% + 5%.

New price = old price take away the discount.

Stage 4 · Loading bay

Make it fit the van

Reflect, rotate or slide each parcel, then paint the squares where it lands.

Learn a bit

Reflect: each square lands the same distance from the mirror, on the other side.

Rotate: turn about the orange centre. 90° is a quarter turn, 180° a half turn.

Translate: slide by a vector. Nothing turns or flips.

Stage 5 · Landing pads

Tile the roof

Turn and flip one tile shape to cover a roof pad with no gaps and no overlaps.

Learn a bit

Shapes tessellate when copies fit together with no gaps and no overlaps.

Here every copy is the same shape. You can turn it and flip it before you place it.

Stage 6 · Describe the move

Tell the robot exactly

The sorting robot needs the full description: what kind of move, and every detail.

Learn a bit

A full description names the move and gives all its details.

A half turn (180°) ends up the same whichever way you turn, so it does not need a direction.
