A language, not a code

Six things worth knowing before you start.
🗣️

BSL is its own language

It has its own grammar and word order. It is not English done with your hands, which is why phrases come out in a different order.

⚖️

It is recognised in law

The British Sign Language Act 2022 recognised BSL as a language of England, Wales and Scotland.

🤨

Your face is grammar

Eyebrows, mouth and head movements are part of the sign. Eyebrows down for a "what" or "where" question, eyebrows up for a yes or no question.

🗺️

Signs have accents

Signs change from region to region, the way spoken words do. That is why the dictionary can show you more than one version.

✌️

Two hands to spell

BSL fingerspelling uses both hands. It is used for names and for words that do not have a sign yet.

📅

Why now

The International Week of Deaf People is the last full week of September, and 23 September is the International Day of Sign Languages.

Deaf with a capital D is often used for people who see themselves as part of the Deaf community, with its own language and culture. To get a Deaf person's attention, a wave or a gentle tap on the shoulder works. Keep your face where they can see it, and there is no need to shout.
