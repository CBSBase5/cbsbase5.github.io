You are about to shrink down and travel inside a computer. Ride along with a key press, cook in the processor's kitchen, juggle the desk, take parts out to see what breaks, build computers for customers, and visit a museum of old tech.

Seven stages, any order, skip anything you like. It saves as you go.

Stage 1 · all aboard

The ride

First, a look at the desk from normal size. Then shrink down, hop in the pod, and ride a key press or a mouse click through the computer. Every part tells you its job when you pull in.

Stage 2 · the processor's kitchen

The CPU kitchen

Inside the processor, it is like a very fast kitchen. The chef (the CPU) follows a recipe (a program) one step at a time, over and over: fetch the next step, decode it, execute it.

Fetch: get the next instruction from memory (RAM).

Decode: work out what the instruction means.

Execute: do it.

A real CPU does this billions of times every second.

Try a bit Cook the order

You are the chef's brain. Press the right button for each step. Take as long as you like.

Try a bit more Jay's toastie recipe has a bug

A CPU does exactly what it is told, in exactly that order, even when it is silly. Jay wrote this recipe in a hurry. Run it to see what happens, then use the arrows to fix the order.

Toy Hire more chefs

A processor can have several cores. Each core is like another chef. Eight pizza orders come in. Slide to hire more chefs.

Some jobs cannot be split between chefs, so more cores does not always mean faster. For lots of separate jobs, it helps a lot.

Stage 3 · the office

Desk and filing cabinet

RAM is the desk. Whatever you are using right now sits on it, so the CPU can grab it fast. The desk only has so much room. When the power goes off, the desk is wiped clean.

Storage is the filing cabinet (an SSD or hard disk drive). It is bigger and slower to get things from, but it keeps everything, even with no power.

Try a bit Juggle the desk

Pick a job. Tap a drawer to get what the job needs out onto the desk. Tap something on the desk to put it away. Everything a job needs has to be on the desk at the same time.

The desk: RAM

The filing cabinet: storage

Try a bit more Desk or cabinet?

Is each card about RAM (the desk) or storage (the cabinet)? Tap a card, then tap where it goes. Or drag it.

Stage 4 · the lab

What if it was gone?

A good way to find out what a part does: take it away and see what stops working. Pick a part to pull out, guess what happens, then switch on to see.

Stage 5 · the build desk

Spec match

Three customers, three very different jobs, three budgets. Pick the parts to build each one a computer that does their job, without spending more than they have.

Every build comes with a case, motherboard and power supply for £150. You choose the rest.

More cores means more working out at once. More RAM means more open at once. An SSD loads things much faster than a hard disk drive. A graphics card draws detailed pictures fast.

Stage 6 · the museum

Then and now museum

Computer parts have changed a lot. The jobs have stayed the same: something still has to get information in, work it out, keep it, and show it.

Try a bit Which came first?

Each case has an old version and a new version of a part. Tap the older one to read its plaque.

Try a bit more Be the curator

The museum has three galleries. Hang each exhibit in the right one. Tap an exhibit, then a gallery. Or drag it.

Make something Write a museum label

Optional. Pick an exhibit and write its label. The chips can start your sentences.
