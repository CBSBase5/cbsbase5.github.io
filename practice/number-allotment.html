THE NUMBER ALLOTMENT

Squares, fractions, sequences

An empty plot that fills up as you work: a patio, seedlings, sunflowers, beds and a harvest. Dig in anywhere.

What this is. Seven jobs on an allotment. Each one has a little bit to learn, something to play with, then a few to try.

Any job, any order, skip whatever is not for you. Your plot fills in as you go, and it saves itself on this device. When you are ready, you can send what you did to Jay.

Who is this?

Optional. Pick your initials and the jobs that suit you best get a badge.

The allotment robin says: any job, any order. A tick appears, and that part of the plot fills in, once you have had a go.

Job 1

The Square Patio

Lay square patios to order and spot square numbers on seed packets.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

A square patio has the same number of slabs across and down.

6 across and 6 down is 6 × 6 = 36 slabs. We write 6² and say “6 squared”.

Going backwards: 36 slabs make a 6 by 6 square, so the square root of 36 is 6. We write √36 = 6.

Job 2

The Big Patio

Square two digit numbers by counting the patio in strips of 10 rows.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

A 27 by 27 patio has 27 rows, with 27 slabs in each row.

Count it in strips. 10 rows is 270 slabs. Another 10 rows is 270 more. The last 7 rows are 7 × 27 = 189.

270 + 270 + 189 = 729, so 27² = 729.

Job 3

Seedling Rows

Rows that grow in a pattern. Find the next and missing rows, then plant from a seed packet rule.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

These rows have 3, 5, 7 seedlings. Each row has 2 more than the row before.

“Add 2 each time” is the term to term rule. Each number in the list is a term.

So the next row will have 7 + 2 = 9.

Try a bit

What comes up?

Some rows are still seeds. How many seedlings will come up there? Picking the rule is optional.

Try a bit more

Plant from the packet

Each seed packet has a planting rule. Plant rows 1 to 4 to match it. In a rule like 3n + 1, n is the row number.

Job 4

The Growth Chart

Write the nth term for a growing plant, predict far ahead, and make your own chart.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

A seedling is 6, 10, 14, 18 cm tall in weeks 1, 2, 3, 4. It grows 4 cm a week.

So start with 4n: that gives 4, 8, 12, 16. Every height is 2 more, so add 2.

The nth term is 4n + 2. In week 10 it is 4 × 10 + 2 = 42 cm.

Try a bit

Read the chart

Write the nth term for each chart. Any way of writing it that works is fine, like 4n + 2 or 2 + 4n.

Use it

Look into the future

Use the rule to find any week, or to check if a height ever happens exactly.

Make one

Your own plant

Invent a plant. Set how tall it is in week 1 and how much it grows each week. Then write its nth term.

Job 5

The Plot Divider

Beds split into plots: matching, planting to match, taking fences out and lining beds up.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

This bed is split into 3 plots and 2 are planted: 2/3 planted.

Put a fence down the middle of every plot. Now there are 6 plots and 4 are planted: 4/6. Same amount of bed.

Fractions that show the same amount are equivalent. Taking fences out again gets back to the simplest form.

Try a bit

Matching beds

Tap two beds with the same amount planted. Find all four pairs.

Try a bit

Plant to match

Plant the new bed so it has the same amount planted as the old one. Tap plots to plant or unplant them.

Try a bit more

Fewest fences

Try taking fences out. A choice only works if no section ends up only part planted. Then write the simplest form.

Line them up

Least to most

Tap the beds in order, from least planted to most planted.

Job 6

The Watering Cans

Top heavy fractions and mixed numbers, poured out one jug at a time.

Good place to start
Learn a bit

The jug holds a third of a can. 7 jugs is 7/3 of a can. The top is bigger than the bottom, so it is top heavy (improper).

3 jugs fill a can. 7 jugs fill 2 cans with 1 jug left over.

So 7/3 = 2 1/3 cans. That is a mixed number: whole cans and a bit.
