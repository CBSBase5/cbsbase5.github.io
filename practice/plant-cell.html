Everything alive is built out of cells. Plant cells are the ones with extra equipment, because a plant has to make its own food and hold itself upright without a skeleton, which is a harder brief than the one you were given.

Section two

What they actually do

Names are the boring half. Here is what each one is for, which is the half that makes the names stick.

Cell wallA stiff box round the outside, made of the same stuff as celery strings. Animal cells do not have one, which is why you are floppy and a tree is not.
Cell membraneThe bouncer. A thin skin just inside the wall that decides what gets in and what gets out.
VacuoleAn enormous bag of water in the middle. When it is full it pushes outwards and the plant stands up. When it is not, the plant flops. That is all wilting is.
NucleusThe one with the instructions in it. Every plan for the whole plant, kept in one purple blob.
ChloroplastThe solar panels. Green, because they absorb every colour except green and fling that one back at your eyes. Plants are not green. Green is the colour plants reject.
CytoplasmThe jelly everything else floats about in. Unglamorous and completely necessary.
MitochondrionThe power station, which turns food into usable energy. You have these too, in every cell, right now.

Section three, optional

How a plant eats, if you want it

This bit is not on the worksheet. Skip it with no consequences whatsoever. It is here because labelling a chloroplast without knowing what it does is a waste of a chloroplast.

A plant builds its own food out of air, water and sunlight. It pulls carbon dioxide in through tiny holes on the underside of its leaves, drinks water up through its roots, and uses light as the power supply. What comes out is sugar, which it lives on, and oxygen, which it throws away as waste.

The oxygen you just breathed in is plant rubbish. Every breath you have ever taken has been somebody else's waste product. Trees are not being generous, they are taking the bins out.
