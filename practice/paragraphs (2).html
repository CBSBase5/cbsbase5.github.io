Paragraph Dash

English

Paragraphs, in four short parts. Answer a bit, play a bit. Jay is in the corner, waiting to be launched at some spikes.

How this works

Every question you have a go at gives Jay a shield.

The game floats in the corner. Play it whenever you like. Each question you check, or ask to be told, drops a shield into Jay's bank, and a shield saves him from one crash. Having a go at a whole part opens the next world. Getting it right is nice, but it is not what opens the worlds.

Drag the game anywhere by its top bar, make it bigger, or shrink it down to a bubble. Everything saves on this device as you go.

The basics

What a paragraph is for, and the sentence that runs it.

Learn a bit

One paragraph, one idea

A paragraph is a box of sentences that are all about the same thing. When the idea changes, you start a new box.

It makes the page easier to read, and it shows the reader where each new idea starts. Nobody enjoys a wall of words.

Learn a bit

The topic sentence is the headline

It tells you what the paragraph is going to be about. It usually comes first.

Every sentence after it backs it up with details. If a sentence does not back it up, it probably belongs somewhere else.

Learn a bit

Linking words are signposts

They show how one idea connects to the next. Some show order, some show a change of direction.

When to start a new paragraph

TiPToP, and the sentence that does not belong.

Learn a bit

TiPToP: start a new paragraph when one of these changes

TiTime
later, the next day
PPlace
kitchen to forest
ToTopic
a new idea or action
PPerson
someone new, or speaking

In GeometJay Dash, every portal is one of these. Go through one and you are in a new paragraph: a Time portal speeds things up, a Place portal moves the scene, a Topic portal flips gravity, and a Person portal shrinks Jay.

Learn a bit

Every sentence has to earn its place

A paragraph sticks to its topic. If one sentence is about something else entirely, it confuses the reader and it has to go.

Sorting it out

Splitting a big block, and putting things in order.

Learn a bit

Split where the idea changes

Read it through and ask, sentence by sentence: is this still about the same thing? The moment the topic, time or place moves on, that is where the new paragraph starts.

Learn a bit

Time words keep events in order

Early, Next, Then, After that, Finally. They tell the reader what happened first and what came after, so nobody gets lost on the way.

Your two paragraphs

A visit to an animal park or a zoo. Real, remembered or made up.

The task

Two paragraphs. One about arriving, one about later.

Paragraph 1 is what you saw and heard when you arrived. Paragraph 2 is later in the day, somewhere different in the park or at an animal show. Give each one a clear topic sentence, and use linking words to join your ideas. Jay marks this one, out of 8.

Linking words. Tap one to drop it in where your cursor is.

Paragraph 1

Arriving

What could you see and hear as soon as you got there? Start with a topic sentence that sets the scene.

Paragraph 2

Later that day

Time has moved on and you are somewhere else. That is TiPToP doing its job. Open with a time or place change, then tell the reader what happened.

It can count and look for words. It cannot tell if your writing is any good, that bit is Jay.
