RIVER ROAD

Physical world

One river, 200 years of people. Weirs, drains, houses, pipes and plastic. What did we do to it, and what could we do next?

Meet the River Fennick. A made up river in an English valley. Follow it from quiet farmland in the 1800s, through mills and factories, new houses and a road, to today. Then you get to design its future.

Six stages, any order. Skip anything that is not for you today. One of them takes you outside for a real walk round school.

Stage 2

Four jars

Twelve things people did to the river. Is each one good, bad, both, or does it depend?

Learn a bit

Both and depends are not the same

Both: it always brings something good and something bad at the same time.

Depends: it could be fine or harmful, depending on how much, when, or how people do it.

Tap a card, then tap a jar. On a computer you can drag too. Changed your mind? With nothing in your hand, tap a card in a jar to take it back out.

Stage 3

River lab

Switch things on and off. Watch the water change, and see which creatures move in or move out.

Learn a bit

Creatures are clues

Scientists check a river by looking at the tiny creatures living in it. Some only live in very clean water. Some can cope with dirty water.

So the creatures tell you how healthy the river is.

Stage 4

Where do the homes go?

The valley needs 40 new homes. You make the calls, then send in a big storm and see what happens.

Learn a bit

The floodplain

The flat land beside a river is its floodplain. When the river is too full, it spreads out over it. That is normal: it is what the land is for.

Grass, trees and soil soak rain up. Tarmac and concrete do not, so rain runs off them fast.
