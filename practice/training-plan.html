    About the six pack

    Since somebody always asks.

    Everyone already has the muscles. They are called the abdominals and they sit in a sheet down the front of your middle, in sections, which is where the six comes from. Training makes them stronger whether or not you can see them.

    Whether they show on the outside is mostly down to things training does not control: how your body is built, what stage of growing you are at, and where your body happens to store fat. Two people can do the same routine for a year and only one of them ends up with visible abs. That is not about effort.

    So it makes a poor goal, because you could do everything right and still not get it. Strength you can actually measure. Pick something like holding a plank for a minute or getting ten press-ups in a row, and train for that instead. The abs get stronger either way.

    If you want to talk about this properly rather than read a box about it, Jay is around.

    Which days

    Muscles get stronger on the days off rather than the days on, so the gaps are part of the plan rather than a gap in it.

    Before and after. A few minutes of moving about before you start, so nothing is cold: walking, star jumps, swinging your arms around. A few minutes of slow stretching afterwards. If something hurts in a sharp way rather than an achy way, stop. That is the whole of the safety talk.
