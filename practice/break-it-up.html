BREAK IT UP

English · paragraphs

Make a mini zine. Every page needs its paragraphs sorting: cut, stick, slice, link and lay it out.

What this is. A mini zine is a little homemade magazine, folded from one sheet and photocopied. This one has seven pages, and every page has a paragraph job. Any order, skip anything, and it saves as you go.

Who is this?

Optional. Pick your initials to see good pages to start on, or just have a look around.

The flat plan: one sheet, eight panels

PAGE1

Cut and stick

Loose sentences, a pair of scissors and some glue. Stick each one on the page where it belongs.

Learn a bit

Same thing, same paragraph

A paragraph is a group of sentences about one thing. On a zine page, each section is about one thing too.

If a sentence is about something else, it goes somewhere else. Sometimes it does not belong at all.

Have a go

Tap a strip, then tap the page it belongs on. You can drag strips too. One strip in each pile does not belong in the zine at all.

Not for you right now? That is fine.Skip to page 2
PAGE2

Top line hunt

These paragraphs lost their first line. Match each one with its topic sentence.

Learn a bit

The top line

A topic sentence usually comes first. It tells the reader what the whole paragraph is about.

A good one is not too vague (“Music is good”) and not about something else.

Have a go

Tap a top line, then tap the dashed space above the paragraph it fits. Some lines are spares that fit nowhere.

If you fancy Write a top line yourself

Skip anything that is not for you.Skip to page 3
PAGE3

The slicer

A wall of text. Slice it into paragraphs and watch the page reflow.

Learn a bit

TiP ToP

Start a new paragraph when there is a new Time, a new Place, a new Topic or a new Person.

Each break you find earns a sticker spot. Stick on the sticker that explains it.

Have a go

Tap a dashed circle between sentences to slice there. Changed your mind? Tap Join back. When it looks right, check the page.

No slicing today? No problem.Skip to page 4
PAGE4

Link bridge

Two paragraphs, one gap. Lay the right plank and Bonnie the poodle can trot across.

Learn a bit

Linking words are bridges

The start of a new paragraph can tell the reader how it connects to the last one.

On top of that adds more. However changes direction. By midnight moves time on. As a result shows what happened because of it.

Have a go

Tap a plank, then tap the gap. Dragging works too. Six bridges, easy to tricky, and one where you make your own plank.

Skip it if it is not for you.Skip to page 5
PAGE5

Shuffled strip

The comic strip page got photocopied out of order. Put the paragraphs back.

Learn a bit

Clues at the start

Each paragraph in a story often starts with a clue: a time (Ten minutes later), a place, or a new person.

Those clues tell you the order, and they make the story flow.

Have a go

Tap one frame, then tap another to swap them. Check whenever you like. Frames in the right place get a pin.

Rather skip this one? Go for it.Skip to page 6
PAGE6

Layout desk

A page about bird watching. Drop each clipping into its box on the layout.

Learn a bit

A box for each paragraph

A clear page has an opening, a paragraph for each part, and an ending.

Subheadings are like signposts: each one says what its paragraph is about.

Have a go

Tap a clipping, then tap the box on the page where it fits. You can drag on a computer too.

Skip to the last page whenever you like.Skip to page 7
